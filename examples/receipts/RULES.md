# Receipt rules (receipts-v2)

Generated by `scripts/fetch_bench_data.py`; edit the script, never this file.

## Prompt rules

The spec and every plain prompt quote this block word for word. Its sha256 (UTF-8 bytes, no trailing newline) is `b552ab6033626d0e5f1792a560932b764b749a7dc17f7f2cbe928653a8f52480`, and every manifest records it.

```text
Return one JSON object with exactly these four keys: total, subtotal, tax, item_count.
- total: the total amount printed on the receipt, including any tax and service, on a line such as TOTAL, GRAND TOTAL, TL or PAID. A line that names a tender (cash, card, e-wallet, voucher), a payment discount or change is never the total. If no total is printed and nothing after the subtotal changes the amount (only tender and change lines, or a 0% discount), the subtotal is the total and subtotal is null.
- subtotal: the first amount printed after the items that sums them, apart from the total, whatever the receipt calls it: SUBTOTAL, ITEMS TOTAL, HARGA JUAL, DPP, NET TOTAL, the amount on an ITEMS line, a TOTAL line that a later GRAND TOTAL follows, or a bare amount under the items. A sum for one group of items (FOOD, BEVERAGE) is not the subtotal.
- tax: the tax amount printed on the receipt (TAX, VAT, PPN, PB1, PAJAK), not its rate, its base (DPP) or a service charge.
- item_count: the number of items listed on the receipt. Count each listed item once, whatever its quantity; the same item on two separate item lines counts twice. Bags and charges listed as items (plastic bag, take-away charge) count, even at price 0. Lines that belong to an item (options, toppings, extras, notes, package contents) are part of that item, even with their own quantity and price. Discount lines are not items. A void line that cancels an item is not an item; the cancelled item still counts.
- Amounts are integers: take the amount itself, without its label or rate, and delete every character that is not a digit 0-9. For example "75,000" is 75000, "Rp 12.500" is 12500, "-2.727" is 2727, "226,500.00" is 22650000 and "PB1 10% 4,818" is 4818.
- An amount the receipt does not print is null; never compute it from other amounts. A printed value without any digit, such as "-", is null.
- If the receipt prints the same field more than once, use the first (topmost) one.
```

The input is `{"receipt": "<text>"}` and the output holds the four keys above; `task.json` has both JSON Schemas. A case is correct only when all four values are exactly equal.

## Labels

Each label is read from the receipt's CORD `gt_parse` annotation:

| Key | Annotation |
|---|---|
| `total` | `gt_parse.total.total_price` |
| `subtotal` | `gt_parse.sub_total.subtotal_price` |
| `tax` | `gt_parse.sub_total.tax_price` |
| `item_count` | the number of entries in `gt_parse.menu` |

1. `total` and `sub_total` may be one object or a list of objects, and a field may be one string or a list of strings when it was annotated more than once. Annotations are read in annotation order, which runs top to bottom on the receipt (`first_not_topmost` checks it).
2. An annotation's amount is the integer left after deleting every character that is not an ASCII digit 0-9. An annotation without a digit has no amount.
3. A field's label is the amount of its first annotation that has one. It is `null` when the field is not annotated or none of its annotations has a digit.
4. `item_count` counts menu entries: a single object counts as 1 and a missing menu as 0. Quantities (`cnt`) and sub-items (`sub`, inside their entry) do not change it. A void line (`void_menu`, kept apart from `menu`) is not counted, while the entry it cancels stays in `menu` and counts.

## Text

The input is one text blob per receipt, rebuilt from the annotated words (`valid_line`), never from the image:

1. Every word of every `valid_line` entry takes part, key words included. Whitespace runs inside a word become one space; a word left empty is dropped.
2. Words are grouped into rows by `row_id`.
3. A word sits at the centre of its quad, the mean of its four x and its four y coordinates. Its box is the smallest upright rectangle around the quad.
4. Two words of one row are stacked when their boxes overlap horizontally by more than 30% of the narrower box and their centres lie more than half the shorter box's height apart vertically. A row with a stacked pair spans several printed lines, and it is split:
   - Levels follow the tilt of the photo: a word's level is its centre y minus the tilt times its centre x. The tilt is the median, over the rows without a stacked pair, of the slope from the row's leftmost word centre to its rightmost; a row whose two ends share a centre x does not count, and the tilt is 0 when no row does.
   - The row holds as many lines as its largest stack, a set of words every two of which are stacked. The words of each largest stack go, by level from the top, to lines 1 to n.
   - Every other word, in annotation order, joins the line whose stack words have the mean level nearest its own (the upper line on a tie), but never a line that holds a word it is stacked with.

   This repairs the receipts where CORD gave one `row_id` to words on several printed lines (16 of CORD's receipts, flagged `row_split`).
5. Lines are ordered by the mean centre y of their words, then by their leftmost centre x, then by `row_id`, then top to bottom within a split row. Words in a line are ordered by centre x, then by annotation order. Positions are compared as exact fractions.
6. Words are joined by one space and lines by one newline, with no trailing newline.

CORD v2 annotates only menu, subtotal, total and void lines (store, date and payment details were never released), so the text holds only those lines.

## Duplicates

CORD holds some receipts more than once, sometimes in two official splits, annotated again with small differences. Receipts are grouped, transitively, when

- their texts hold the same multiset of lowercase letter and digit runs (case, punctuation, spacing and line breaks do not matter),
- their texts hold the same sequence of digits (the same amounts in the same order; the four labels are read from those amounts), or
- a hand review found them to be copies of one purchase that neither key joins: the texts differ only by line breaks, an item or option name, or a line one copy lacks, and every amount both print agrees, cash and change included. The 16 pairs are listed in the script and under `near_duplicates` in `manifests/excluded.json`, with what differs. Repeat purchases of the same order, which differ in the cash tendered or the way of paying, stay separate receipts.

A group whose members carry different labels is dropped whole (`label_conflict`): two annotations of one receipt disagree, so it has no single answer. Any other group keeps one case: a test copy first, then validation, then train, and the lowest row index within a split. The other copies are dropped (`duplicate`). `manifests/excluded.json` lists every dropped receipt with its reason.

## Exclusions

Some receipts are left out by hand before the splits are assigned (the list is in the script, from the label audit in AUDIT.md). Each carries a reason and a note in `manifests/excluded.json`:

| Reason | Meaning | Receipts |
|---|---|---|
| `label_wrong` | a label contradicts the printed text | 15 |
| `rule_conflict` | a label follows a CORD habit that the prompt rules settle the other way | 20 |
| `garbled_text` | the rebuilt text pairs amounts with the wrong lines, because CORD put them in the row_id of a neighbouring line | 3 |

A duplicate group with a listed member is dropped whole, its unlisted copies noted as copies. When the copies of a listed receipt disagree on a label, the group is recorded as `label_conflict` and the listed receipt keeps its note.

## Splits

1. Groups made only of train receipts are ordered by the sha256 (hex) of the kept case's text.
2. The first 150 are the pilot, the next 100 the rehearsal set and the next 200 the evidence floor.
3. The delivery set is every other train group plus every group with a validation or test copy.
4. The rehearsal set and the evidence floor are Architect floors. Architect sorts their case ids and gives even positions to tuning and odd positions to the held-out exam (`architect/floors/base.py`), so they split 50/50 and 100/100.

No two sets share a case or a duplicate group, hand-joined copies included. A case id is `cord-<split>-<row>`: the CORD split and the row index within it.

## Flags

Flags are diagnostics and never change a label. Each manifest lists a case's flags, and `stats.json` lists the cases under each flag. `<f>` names the label concerned.

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `multi_value:<f>` | annotated more than once, every time with the same amount |
| `multi_value_disagree:<f>` | annotated more than once with different amounts; the first is the label |
| `first_not_topmost:<f>` | the first annotation is not the topmost printing |
| `printing_mismatch:<f>` | the annotations could not be matched to their printed lines |
| `multi_number:<f>` | the annotation holds more than one number (`Pb1 10% 7,000`, or `154 000` with a space between thousands), so deleting the non-digits joins them |
| `decimal:<f>` | the amount ends in one or two decimals (such as `226,500.00`), which stay as digits |
| `sign:<f>` | the amount carries a minus sign or a parenthesis |
| `no_digit:<f>` | annotated without any digit (such as `-`), so the label is null |
| `total_missing` | no `total_price` annotation, so `total` is null |
| `total_missing_total_word` | no `total_price` annotation, yet a line says total (not subtotal) beside an amount of four or more digits |
| `sub_items` | a menu entry has sub-items |
| `menu_entry_without_name` | a menu entry has no name |
| `quantity_over_1` | a menu entry has a quantity above 1 |
| `void_menu` | the receipt has a void line |
| `row_split` | a `row_id` spans several printed lines, so its row was split (Text, step 4) |

Hand-check the suspect flags first: `multi_value_disagree`, `multi_number`, `first_not_topmost`, `printing_mismatch`, `total_missing_total_word`, `menu_entry_without_name`.
